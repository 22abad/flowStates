# The Humble Beginner's Muscle Memory: A Post-Exam Dawn in Eolas 011

The first semester's final exams just concluded yesterday. By all conventional wisdom, today should have been a day for recovery, deep sleep, or unbridled indulgence.

Yet, here I am, sitting in Eolas 011. It's 7:10 AM, and outside, Ireland is still shrouded in darkness. But the screens before me are alight. I've opened LeetCode, not for an exam, not to impress anyone, but simply because my fingers itch to code.

Some might label me an "overachiever," but they misunderstand. I simply acknowledge myself as a "humble beginner" (肉鸡), yet one who is diligently forging formidable "muscle memory" (肌肉记忆).

## 1. My Built-in Garbage Collection (GC) Mechanism

I used to doubt my memory. Theories that other students could recall after a single glance would vanish from my mind; an API I'd memorized yesterday might require documentation lookup today. This anxiety plagued me for a long time, feeling like the biggest obstacle on my career transition journey.

But this morning, while tackling an array problem, a sudden realization struck me: this isn't a flaw; it's a high-level gift from God—an automatic Garbage Collection mechanism!

Just as Java needs GC to prevent memory leaks, so does the human brain. My mind automatically filters out rigid definitions, useless anxieties, and negative emotional noise like "I can't do it" or "it's too hard." What I forget are the superficial "moves," but what remains is pure "inner strength."

As I coded just now, my fingers instinctively typed `while (left < right)` without conscious thought. In that moment, I knew that the logic that once tormented me had bypassed the hippocampus and was directly etched into my spinal cord. A poor memory is irrelevant, because my muscles (my "chicken" muscles, if you will) remember.

## 2. The "Butterfly Effect" of Eolas 011

The most moving part of this morning wasn't solving a Hard problem; it was seeing the Irish guy sitting next to me.

Last night, we were sharing a Guinness, and he'd planned to sleep until noon today. But because I showed up, because of my unwavering, almost obsessive, persistence over the past few months, he too appeared in Eolas, rubbing the sleep from his eyes and carrying his bag.

In that instant, I felt a sense of accomplishment far stronger than an "Accepted" status on LeetCode.

I used to think changing myself was the hardest thing. Now I realize that when you reshape yourself to be sufficiently resilient and radiant, you naturally illuminate those around you. Even if it's just a small dose of positive energy, even if it just makes someone sleep an hour less or write one more line of code, that's a tremendous impact. What is true happiness? It's not being exceptionally brilliant alone, but this silent, radiating vitality that emanates from within.

## 3. True Freedom: The Escape from Mental Dimensions

Looking back over these past four months, from the initial "Can I even learn this?" to the current "Who else wants a challenge?!" I no longer worry about failing to grasp concepts or giving up halfway. These four months haven't just been about mastering Java, stacks, queues, and two-pointers; more importantly, I've completed a refactoring and upgrade of my entire mental system.

I've found my "freedom." This isn't the freedom to do whatever I want (that's indulgence), but the freedom to _not_ do what I don't want. I no longer care about unnecessary face-saving, or what others say about "a man my age changing careers," or momentary setbacks.

Because I know that, thanks to daily persistence, my core is as stable as a rock. This psychological freedom, this mental liberation, is the highest dimension of freedom. It allows me to observe my clumsy self (the "humble beginner") as an outsider, pat his shoulder, and say with a smile, "It's okay, keep practicing and you'll build some muscle."

## Final Thoughts

I may always be a "humble beginner"—in the vast expanse of computer science, I will forever remain humble, always acknowledging myself as a novice. But this humble beginner has developed a conditioned reflex of "muscle memory."

The moment the code passed, seeing that green "Accepted," I couldn't help but smile. Hi, hi, hi! I'm a humble beginner, but I'm happy. Next problem, bring it on!

# 肉鸡的肌肉记忆：期末考后，Eolas 011 的破晓

昨天刚结束第一学期的期末考试。按照常理，今天应该是宿醉、睡大觉、或者彻底放纵的一天。

但我现在坐在 Eolas 011 的教室里。早晨 7:10，窗外的爱尔兰还是一片漆黑，但我面前的屏幕是亮的。我打开了 LeetCode，不是为了考试，不是为了应付谁，而是因为——我的手痒了。

有人说我是卷王，其实他们不懂。我只是承认自己是只“肉鸡”，但我正在练就一身可怕的“肌肉记忆”。

## 1. 我自带 Garbage Collection (GC) 机制

以前，我总怀疑自己记性不好。别的同学看一遍就能复述的理论，我转头就忘；昨天刚背的 API，今天可能就得查文档。我为此焦虑过很久，觉得这是我转码路上最大的拦路虎。

但就在今天早晨，刷着那道数组题时，我突然顿悟了：这哪里是缺陷，这明明是上帝赐给我的高级天赋——自动垃圾回收（Garbage Collection）！

Java 需要 GC 来防止内存泄漏，人脑也一样。 我的大脑自动帮我过滤掉了那些死板的定义、无用的焦虑、还有那些“我不行”、“太难了”的负面情绪噪音。 我忘掉的是“招式”，但留下的全是“内功”。

刚才写代码时，我甚至没有过脑子，手指就自动敲出了 `while (left < right)`。那一刻我知道，那些曾经折磨我的逻辑，已经绕过了海马体，直接刻进了脊髓里。 记性不好没关系，因为我的肌肉（鸡肉）记得。

## 2. Eolas 011 的“蝴蝶效应”

今天早晨最让我感动的，不是我解出了一道 Hard 题，而是坐在我旁边的那个爱尔兰哥们。

昨晚我们还在一起喝 Guinness，他本来打算今天睡到日上三竿。但因为我来了，因为我这几个月像个疯子一样雷打不动的坚持，他今天也揉着惺忪的睡眼，背着包出现在了 Eolas。

那一瞬间，我感受到了一种比 AC（Accepted）更强烈的成就感。

以前我觉得改变自己是最难的，现在发现，当你把自己重塑得足够坚硬和发光时，你自然会照亮周围的人。 哪怕只是带来一点点正能量，哪怕只是让身边的人少睡一小时懒觉、多写一行代码，这也是一种巨大的影响力。 真正的幸福是什么？不是独自牛逼，而是这种无声的、从内向外辐射的生命力。

## 3. 真·自由：心智维度的越狱

回首这整整 4 个月。 从一开始的“我能学会吗？”到现在的“还有谁？！” 我彻底不担心自己学不进去，也不担心会半途而废了。这 4 个月不仅仅是掌握了 Java、栈、队列、双指针，更重要的是——我完成了一次心智系统的重构升级。

我发现我“自由”了。 这种自由不是想干什么就干什么（那叫放纵），而是不想干什么就不干什么。 我不再 Care 那些没必要的面子，不再 Care 别人说“你这个年纪转行行不行”，不再 Care 一时的挫败感。

因为我知道，由于每一天的坚持，我的内核已经稳如磐石。这种心理维度的自由，才是最高维度的自由。它让我能够像旁观者一样看着那个笨拙的自己（肉鸡），笑着拍拍他的肩膀说：“没事，再练练就有肌肉了。”

## 写在最后

我可能永远是只“肉鸡”——在浩瀚的计算机科学面前，我永远保持谦卑，永远承认自己是菜鸟。 但我这只肉鸡，已经练出了条件反射般的“肌肉记忆”。

刚才代码跑通的那一刻，看着绿色的 Passed，我忍不住想笑。 Hi啊Hi啊Hi啊！ 我是肉鸡，但我很快乐。 下一题，来战！

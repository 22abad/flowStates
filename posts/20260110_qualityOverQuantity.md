# 173 > 500: The Internalization Breakthrough from a 6-Hour Defeat

**Date:** 2026-01-10
**Location:** Ireland

Today is a day worth documenting.

A moment ago, I independently solved **LeetCode 990 (Satisfiability of Equality Equations)**. No Google, no solution-peeking. There were several gut-wrenching moments where I desperately wanted to ask an AI, but I physically held my hands back, resisting the instinct to take a shortcut.

When the final debug succeeded and the green "Accepted" lit up, I did something I rarely do—I clicked on the comments section. I usually avoid it to prevent my thinking from being contaminated. But today, perhaps fueled by the satisfaction of this solo victory, I wanted to see how other climbers felt.

That's when I saw a comment from a user named **Arc**. He mentioned he had solved 500 problems and was proud to have completed this one independently. He started in June 2023 and reached this point in September 2024—a journey of one year and three months.

Reading his words, I glanced at my own stats:
**Started on October 11, 2025. Today is January 10, 2026. Exactly 3 months. Total problems solved: 173.**

If you just look at the numbers, 173 < 500. But in my view, the "underlying logic" built by these 173 problems is solid enough to make me feel extremely satisfied with my achievement. This isn't boasting; it's a cognitive breakthrough regarding **"quantity" versus "quality."**

### 1. The "Darkest Hour": A 6-Hour Defeat

Let's rewind three months to October 11, 2025.
It was my first LeetCode problem, a standard Easy. I sat in front of the computer—no food, no water, not even a bathroom break—for a full **6 hours**.
And guess what? **I failed.**

The sense of defeat was devastating. I couldn't translate a logic that a human could see in an instant into a language a computer could understand. For most, this would be the moment they quit. But strangely, that defeat didn't crush me. It acted like a shot of adrenaline, igniting a deep desire to conquer this challenge. I thought to myself: **"If this wall is so hard, the view after breaking through it must be spectacular."**

### 2. Redefining "Brushing Problems"

When I started, many "seniors" (including some who had solved 400+ problems but still couldn't find a job) told me: "Just do around 200. Memorize the Easy and Medium ones; that's what they ask in interviews."
I believed them at first. But today, placing my 173 problems on the same scale as Arc's 500, I see the fatal flaw in that logic.

**The essence of problem-solving is not the accumulation of numbers, but the internalization of thinking.**

If we compare problem-solving to constructing a building, many people are just stacking 500 blocks on flat ground, ready to be toppled by a gust of wind. My 173 problems, however, were each about digging a deeper foundation.

I believe being on the "same level" isn't about problem count, but about:

1.  **Clarity of Logical Structure:** Can you articulate the architecture of your solution?
2.  **Versatility:** Can you fluently switch between multiple approaches for the same problem?
3.  **The Ultimate Test:** Can you resist the urge to Google or ask an AI, and independently debug your way to "Accepted"?

My sole objective these past three months has been to achieve the **highest density of skill internalization with the minimum number of problems.** I know that the difference in the underlying logic determines the final height of the building.

I've even set an **"Ultimate Challenge"** for myself:
**Can I master a curated set of 50-70 "mother problems" so thoroughly that I can cover the logical variations of thousands of LeetCode questions?**
In this age of AI, the ability to achieve strong generalization from a minimal sample set is the core human competency. This isn't just for interviews; it's about not wasting life on repetitive labor.

### 3. The Irish "Smoker's Circle": A Real-World Feynman Technique

Beyond code, my life in Ireland has offered a huge revelation.
I used to think that chatting with my Irish friends (and other immigrants) during smoke breaks was a "waste of time." I now see it as a **high-stakes, win-win training ground.**

Here, it's not just me who is a foreigner; often, _everyone_ is a foreigner. None of us are speaking our native tongue. In this increasingly international environment, the **margin for error in communication is extremely low, but the training value is extremely high.**

1.  **Exchanging information** is the surface level.
2.  **Practicing English** is the basic exercise.
3.  **The true core is this:** We are forced to use the **simplest, most universal language (Globish)** to explain complex ideas to people from completely different backgrounds and make them understand.

This is the ultimate real-world application of the **Feynman Technique**. If I can clearly explain a complex algorithm during a smoky, casual chat, I can handle any interviewer with ease. What we are honing in our stumbling conversations is the most precise form of **"low-noise communication"** for a globalized world.

### Final Thoughts

From the 6-hour defeat on my first problem to looking at a 500-problem peer in the comments today, I am filled with confidence in my 173 problems.
These three months, I didn't walk the path of "mass production," but the path of "precision engineering."

I am very satisfied with where I am, but I know this is not the end.
**The challenge to deconstruct a world of thousands of problems with just 50 has only just begun.**

# 173 > 500：从“6 小时溃败”到独立攻克 LC990 的内化突围

**时间：** 2026 年 1 月 10 日
**坐标：** 爱尔兰

今天是一个值得记录的日子。

就在刚才，我独立解决了 **LeetCode 990 (Satisfiability of Equality Equations)**。没有谷歌，没有看题解，更有好几次在那抓心挠肝想问 AI 的时候，我死死地按住了自己的手，忍住了那种“走捷径”的本能。

当我最终 Debug 成功，看着绿色的“Accepted”亮起时，我做了一件平时极少做的事——我点开了评论区。平时我不看评论，是因为不想被干扰，但今天，或许是因为独立解出这道题的成就感，我想去看看其他的攀登者是什么感受。

也就是在那一刻，我看到了一个名叫 **Arc** 的网友的留言。他说他做了 500 道题，非常骄傲能独立完成这道题。他是 23 年 6 月加入的，到 24 年 9 月，用了一年三个月的时间达到了这个高度。

看着他的文字，我看了一眼自己的数据：
**我是 2025 年 10 月 11 日开始的，今天是 2026 年 1 月 10 日。满打满算 3 个月，做题量定格在 173。**

如果光看数字，173 < 500。但在我看来，这 173 道题构建的“底层逻辑”，其坚固程度足以让我对自己的成绩感到极度满意。这不是自夸（Not boasting），而是一场关于**“数量”与“质量”**的认知突围。

### 一、 那个“至暗”的起点：6 小时的溃败

把时间拨回 3 个月前，2025 年 10 月 11 日。那是我 LeetCode 的第一题，一道标准的 Easy。我坐在电脑前，没吃、没喝、甚至没上厕所，整整憋了**6 个小时**。你猜怎么着？**我没做出来。Failed。**

那种挫败感是毁灭性的——我竟然无法将人类一眼就能看穿的逻辑，翻译成计算机能懂的语言。对于大多数人来说，这可能就是“劝退”时刻。但很奇怪，那次溃败没有击垮我，反而像是一针兴奋剂，彻底激发了我挑战的欲望。我当时想：**“既然这堵墙这么硬，那把它撞开后的风景一定很美。”**

### 二、 重新定义“刷题”的底层逻辑

开始刷题时，很多“前辈”（包括一些刷了 400 题却依然没找到工作的人）告诉我：“刷个 200 多道就够了，把 Easy 和 Medium 背熟，那是面试的大头。”我一开始信了。但今天，当我把我的 173 题和 Arc 的 500 题放在同一个天平上时，我发现那个“前辈逻辑”有致命的漏洞。

**刷题的本质，不在于数字的堆砌，而在于思维的“内化”。**

如果我们把解题比作盖楼，很多人是在平地上搭积木，搭了 500 块，风一吹就倒。而我这 173 道题，每一道都是深挖地基。我认为，真正的“同一 Level”不是看题量，而是看：

1.  **逻辑结构是否清晰？**
2.  **是否熟练掌握多种解法并能自如切换？**
3.  **最关键的一点：你是否能忍住不 Google、不问 AI，独立 Debug 直至 AC？**

这 3 个月，我追求的唯一目标，就是**用最少的题量，达成最高密度的技巧内化**。我知道，底层逻辑的差异，注定了最终建筑的高度。

我甚至给自己定下了一个**“终极 Challenge”**：
**如果我能精选出 50-70 道“母题”，把它们彻底吃透，能不能覆盖 LeetCode 几千道题的逻辑变种？**
在这个 AI 时代，极小样本下的强泛化能力，才是人类最核心的竞争力。这不仅是为了面试，更是为了不浪费生命在重复的劳动中。

### 三、 爱尔兰的“抽烟社交”：互为“外国人”的生存智慧

除了代码，这段时间在爱尔兰的生活也给了我巨大的启示。以前我觉得，和那群爱尔兰的朋友（或者其他国家的移民）在室外抽烟闲聊挺“浪费时间”的。但现在我发现，这其实是一个**多赢的高阶训练场**。

在这里，不仅仅我是外国人，大家往往都是“外国人”。我们都没有在使用母语。在这种越来越国际化的环境下（大量移民涌入），**沟通的容错率极低，但训练价值极高**。

1.  **互通有无**只是表层。
2.  **练习英语**是基本功。
3.  **真正的核心是：** 我们必须逼迫自己用**最简单、最通俗的语言（Globish）**，把复杂的想法讲给背景完全不同的人听，并且让他们听懂。

这对沟通能力的要求极高。这不就是**费曼学习法（The Feynman Technique）**的终极实践吗？如果在烟雾缭绕的闲聊中，我能把一个复杂的算法思想讲清楚，那在面试中面对面试官，我就能游刃有余。我们在互相磕磕绊绊中磨练出的，是**全球化语境下最精准的“降噪沟通”能力**。

### 写在最后

从第一题的 6 小时惨败，到今天看着评论区里 500 题的同行，我对自己手中的这 173 题充满了信心。这 3 个月，我走的不是一条“量产”的路，而是一条“精工”的路。

我对自己的现状很满意，但我知道这还不是终点。
**用 50 道题解构几千道题的世界，这个 Challenge 才刚刚开始。**
